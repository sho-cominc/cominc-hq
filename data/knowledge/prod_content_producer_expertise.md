## 1. Threads Algorithm

### How It Works
Not a rule-based system. Meta's AI runs a three-step loop:
1. **Inventory** — what content is eligible to be shown
2. **Signals** — how users typically interact with similar content
3. **Prediction** — what this specific user is likely to do next

Each post gets a probability score per action (reply, repost, dwell time). Highest-probability posts get fed into **For You** (AI-driven, non-followers included). The **Following** feed is strict reverse-chronological.

**Discovery happens almost entirely through For You.** This means niche consistency and engagement velocity are the two levers that matter most.

### Velocity Window
The algorithm watches the **first 30–90 minutes** after posting most closely. A post that gets 20 replies in 30 minutes outperforms one that accumulates 50 replies over 24 hours. Being active and responding to comments immediately after posting is not optional — it is the mechanism.

### Communities & Topic Tags (2025)
Threads launched 200+ topic categories in 2025. Tagging a post to a topic generates more views than untagged posts per Meta's own data. Use it. It's essentially free reach segmentation.

### "Dear Algo" Feature (Feb 2026)
Users can now explicitly tell the algorithm what to show more or less of. Resets every ~3 days. Implication: audience preferences are fluid. If your content suddenly drops in reach, it may be due to audience-side preference resets, not a change in your quality.

## 2. Engagement Signals

### Signal Hierarchy (Ranked by Algorithmic Weight)

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Replies (meaningful) | **Highest** | Cognitive effort = highest weight. Multi-turn threads especially. |
| Quote posts | High | Piggybacks on original post's distribution |
| Reposts | Medium-High | Places content in non-follower feeds |
| Dwell time | Medium | Reading without acting still signals quality |
| Likes | **Low** | Does not drive reach alone |

**Key stat:** Replies now generate ~50% of total impressions platform-wide (Meta tip sheet data). Replying to your own post's comments boosts total engagement by **42%** (analysis of 128,000 posts).

### What Suppresses Reach
- Low engagement rate (small follower count but nobody replies)
- Inconsistent niche — the AI can't categorize you, so it distributes you to nobody
- Posting volume without quality — the AI penalizes filler
- Ignoring comments — posts with no author reply get less algorithmic extension

## 3. Japanese Market (Threads JP)

### Market Reality
- ~13 million Japanese Threads users (late 2025)
- Japan has more X (Twitter) daily users per capita than any country in the world — Threads is a challenger, not dominant
- **Early-mover advantage is real**: Threads ads only launched in Japan in April 2025; organic window is still open
- Demographics: 57% female, 43% male; Gen Z and Millennials are the core
- Meta is actively investing in Japan: Japan-local trending topics feature, Japan Threads account launched

### How Japanese Users Experience Threads
- Described as "equal-footed self" — they post thoughts they wouldn't put on curated Instagram
- Platform feel: "overhearing café conversations" or "listening to radio"
- Authenticity and relatability are valued above professional polish
- Cross-platform integration: sharing Instagram posts to Threads gives ~1.5x reach amplification (LIDDELL Corp data)

### What Performs Well in JP
- **Personal stories and daily life moments** — small, relatable observations
- **Behind-the-scenes and process content** — "how this actually works"
- **Short, witty one-liners** — humor works, but accessibility over sophistication
- **Trend participation** — Japanese users are highly responsive to trending local topics (Threads now surfaces Japan-local trends)
- **Warm community storytelling** — not brand announcements

### Tone Rules for JP
- **Casual, not formal** — keigo reads as cold and corporate, kills engagement
- Self-deprecating honesty performs well
- Direct promotional language is actively disliked
- Short-to-medium length beats long-form for most content

### Hashtags in JP
- Hashtags on Threads are **AI-sorting signals**, not browse-by-discovery pathways
- Use **3–5 specific, relevant hashtags** integrated naturally into the post
- Do NOT append 20+ hashtags at the end (Instagram-style) — reads as spam
- Niche hashtags outperform broad ones

### JP Reply Culture
Japanese Threads users return to accounts where the creator responds to every comment. Building a JP presence is fundamentally about becoming a recognizable voice in a small community before attempting broad reach. Warm, consistent replies = foundation.

## 4. Foreign / English-Speaking Market

### Platform State
- Threads reached **400M MAU / 115M DAU** (late 2025) — strong growth trajectory
- EN audience is conversation-first, dislikes broadcast-style posting
- Described as "the last platform where accounts can go 0 → 100K without algorithmic suppression"
- Healthy engagement rate benchmark: **4–5%**

### Hooks That Work for EN Audiences
- **Bold opening statements** — short, strong opinions, contrarian takes
- **Curiosity gaps** — "Here's what nobody tells you about X"
- **Relatable frustrations** — "Every [type of person] knows this feeling"
- **Data in the first line** — numbers stop the scroll
- **Specific answer questions** — "Which would you pick: A or B?" beats generic "thoughts?"
- Informal, human-first voice throughout

### Travel & Tourism Content on Threads (EN)
Threads does not have a strong travel ecosystem like Instagram/TikTok, but traction is possible:
- **POV-style narrative hooks** — pull the reader into the experience
- **Micro-observations** — specific sensory details beat "Japan is amazing"
- **Honest takes** — "The one thing travel guides won't tell you about X"
- **Behind-the-scenes of running a small travel business** — outperforms pure destination content
- Local/founder voice outperforms polished brand voice

### Cross-Cultural Note
Running bilingual content (JP + EN) on one account creates algorithmic friction — the AI can't categorize your audience clearly, reducing reach in both directions. Workarounds:
- Signal language clearly in the post itself
- Use topic tags to help the AI segment
- Content that works cross-culturally: behind-the-scenes of local life, specific sensory details, honest personal stories — universals that don't need cultural translation

## 5. Multi-Series Content Management

### The Core Risk: Algorithmic Identity Dilution
If an account jumps between unrelated topics, the AI doesn't know who to show it to — reach suffers across all categories. The fix is **content pillars**: a small set of themes that sit under one overarching identity. Each series lives within a pillar. The audience learns the territory; the algorithm learns who to show it to.

### Content Pillars for ComInc. (Proposed)
1. **Team / behind-the-scenes** — who we are, how we work (Arc 1 was this)
2. **Business in motion** — the real work: permits, renovations, launches, setbacks
3. **Place** — Joetsu/Myoko: local life, seasons, culture, hidden spots
4. **Guest / visitor perspectives** — social proof, community, real stories
5. **Practical travel** — useful tips for visiting the area (EN-leaning)

All series belong to one of these pillars. Nothing gets posted outside them.

### Arc Pacing
- **Episodic content performs ~8x better than one-off posts** (Sprout Social)
- **57% of global social media users** want brands to post original content series (2026 survey)
- A predictable weekly cadence creates ritual — audiences return because they expect it
- Each episode should be **standalone-valuable** while rewarding those following the full arc

**Between arcs:**
1. **Wrap post** — explicitly close the previous series ("That's Arc 1 done. Here's what I noticed.")
2. **1–2 interstitial posts** — personal update, behind-the-scenes; re-introduces you as a person, not a content machine
3. **Announcement post** — "Starting [Series Name] next week" — builds anticipation, signals to algorithm

**Gap length:** 3–7 days between series. Too short = no breathing room. Too long = momentum lost.

### "What Happens Next" Tension — Mechanics
- End each post on an **unresolved question or problem**, not a conclusion
- **Numerical framing**: "Day 1 of 30: here's where we're starting" — creates completion anxiety and return visits
- **Community stakes**: "I'll ask you to vote on the next step" — makes the audience co-creators
- Use real-world unresolved events as built-in cliffhangers (permit pending, launch date approaching, first guest incoming)

### Series Labeling Convention
Start each series post with an explicit label in the first line:
```
[Series Name] #1:
[Series Name] — Part 2 of 5:
```
This trains both the reader and the algorithm. Consistency in the label format matters.

## 6. Reply & Community Tactics

### The Velocity Rule
Reply to **every comment within the first 60–90 minutes** after posting. This is the window where the algorithm decides how broadly to distribute. Once the window closes, replies still matter for relationships but have lower algorithmic impact.

### Reply Quality > Reply Volume
Threads' AI now weights the **quality** of replies, not just quantity. Short emoji replies from commenters are filtered. Questions that prompt one-word answers are less effective than questions that prompt opinions. When you reply, write full sentences — your reply is a new engagement event.

### Advanced Reply Tactics
- **Reply to your own post** to re-surface it in feeds (works as a bump)
- **Quote-post a commenter's reply** with your additional take — creates a second distribution event off the original post
- **Reference previous commenters** ("you mentioned X last week...") — builds personal loyalty
- **Acknowledge regulars** publicly — repeat engagers are your distribution engine

### Community Building Phases

**Phase 1: Core Community (0–1K followers)**
- Reply to 100% of comments
- Goal: 20–50 people who reliably engage on every post
- These people ARE your distribution engine
- Do not pivot to promotional content — protect the community tone

**Phase 2: Broadcast Mode (1K+ followers)**
- Community from Phase 1 now functions as social proof
- Shift to quality replies over 100% coverage
- Focus on niche consistency and trend participation
- Broad reach comes from: Communities/topic tags, quote-posting viral content, trending topic participation

**Critical insight:** Threads actively suppresses accounts that look like broadcasters without community. An account with 500 followers and 15% engagement rate gets more For You distribution than 10,000 followers at 0.5%.
